1. Read `docs/STATE.md`, `docs/ENGINEERING.md`, `docs/RESEARCH.md`, and the latest `docs/reports/` report before acting. These are the canonical handoff and decision records. Do not rely on previous chat memory.
2. **Use the user-supplied theoretical ideas catalog as an ACTIVE EXPERIMENT QUEUE**, especially once incremental optimization of a current path has diminishing returns. Do not mistake it for a list to dismiss because similar ideas were historically attempted. Consult `docs/research/attempted-ideas.md` separately as the retrospective record of concrete prior implementations, compare scope, and attempt materially new variants of the catalog's theories on original Silesia bytes. Maintain a durable per-theory status and measured accept/reject evidence under `docs/research/theory-backlog.md` and `docs/reports/`. Historical negative prototypes or external-compressor diagnostics do not themselves veto new investigations. Always preserve native standalone byte-exact JLZ.
3. **All lasting source, documentation, and workflow edits are made directly on `main`. No feature, research, or PR branches.** The sole normal exception is the ephemeral `jls-test/*` branch used to dispatch Test against `main`, automatically deleted by the trigger workflow. Local disposable experiment directories are allowed, but never create persistent research branches.
4. **Approved baseline is Round 29** (2026-10-18): 62,644,663 B complete Silesia, ten candidate-only whole-corpus median encode 7.516364 s and decode 2.821363 s. Against retained accepted Round 25: 63,104,117 B, 7.549920 s, 2.839905 s. The current/previous JLZ timing comparison is across independent measurement sessions, not new paired benchmarks; the ~0.4/0.65% speed differences are suggestive, not definitive. Full canonical report: docs/reports/2026-10-18-round29.md.
5. **YAGNI always.** Implement only demonstrably necessary scope. Prefer simple, measurable approaches and delete unused local experiments and rejected candidate code after documenting results. Comment non-obvious code, including invariants, bitstream layouts, compatibility decisions, and mathematical derivations; avoid tautological comments.
6. JLZ must remain one fixed automatic encoding procedure and exactly two CLI verbs, `compress` and `decompress`. No levels, optional user codec modes or ad-hoc flags. Internal automatically selected representations are allowed. Maintain `JLZ1` backward decode where practical; clearly report format incompatibilities before acceptance.
7. Never use DEFLATE, Zstd, XZ, Brotli, LZMA, or any existing compressor backend as JLZ's engine, fallback, terminal coder, or production path. Use other codecs **only** as external fixed benchmarks or clearly marked diagnostic transforms. No fake novelty claims; exact matching and standard entropy coding have prior art.
8. Acceptance: original-byte exact roundtrips, complete Silesia archive bytes including metadata, existing approved JLZ and fixed Zstd/XZ references reused (NOT rebenchmarked), and candidate-only median of 10 whole-Silesia encoding/decoding runs for a promotable full-archive candidate. Include per-file/worst regressions and validate with go test -count=1, go vet and appropriate race checks. Do not call a sample/component screen a whole-archive result; no need for expensive ten-run benchmarks when the candidate clearly fails inexpensive viability probes.
9. User generally approves changes that improve aggregate size and encoding time, considering decoding, memory and important per-file regressions. **There is no fixed percentage acceptance threshold.** After a few consecutive diminishing improvements, investigate a meaningfully different structural idea rather than endless parameter tuning. Reject pointless complexity and disproportionate resource costs. Preserve reproducible prior versions.

Test/nightly **completely replace** previous prerelease assets and tags. No executable/binary commits. Consult `docs/WORKFLOWS.md`.

Update `docs/RESEARCH.md` and `docs/STATE.md` before finishing. Record negative experiments too. Do not orphan reports in experiment folders.
